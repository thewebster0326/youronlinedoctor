Guide

Psychologist, psychiatrist or counsellor — who should you see?

The titles get used interchangeably. They are three different jobs.

Deciding to get help is usually the hard part. Then comes a second obstacle nobody warns you about: working out which kind of professional you are actually looking for, with three titles that sound similar and mean different things.

The shortest version: psychiatrists are medical doctors and can prescribe. Psychologists provide assessment and therapy and cannot prescribe. Counsellors provide support and counselling within a narrower scope. All three are registered, and all three are legitimate - for different things.

Psychiatrist

A medical doctor who has specialised in psychiatry. They diagnose mental illness and can prescribe medication, and they are the right person where medication is likely to be central to treatment.

Typically involved in more severe or complex presentations, or where a diagnosis is unclear and needs medical assessment.

Psychologist

A registered professional providing psychological assessment and talking therapy. They do not prescribe.

This is who most people picture when they imagine therapy, and it is where the bulk of ongoing work happens - anxiety, depression, trauma, relationships, burnout.

Counsellor

Registered counsellors work within a defined scope, providing supportive counselling and containment rather than the full range of psychological intervention. They refer on where something falls outside that scope.

Often more accessible and more affordable, and entirely appropriate for many situations.

A reasonable starting point

If you genuinely do not know, starting with a doctor is sensible. A consultation can establish what is going on and point you to the right discipline, rather than you guessing and possibly spending months with the wrong fit.

That is also the honest answer to the cost question. Getting matched correctly first time is cheaper than three false starts.

If you are in crisis

None of this applies. If you are having thoughts of harming yourself, go to your nearest emergency department or contact emergency services immediately. Do not wait for an appointment with anyone.

Rough guide

  • Medication likely needed - psychiatrist
  • Ongoing talking therapy - psychologist
  • Support through a difficult period - counsellor
  • Not sure what is happening - start with a doctor
  • In crisis - emergency department, now

Questions

Common questions

Do psychologists prescribe medication in South Africa?

No. Psychologists provide assessment and therapy. Where medication needs considering, referral to a doctor or psychiatrist is arranged.

Is a psychiatrist a medical doctor?

Yes. A psychiatrist is a medical doctor who has specialised in psychiatry and can prescribe.

Do I need a diagnosis before seeing a psychologist?

No. Many people seek psychological support for a difficult period rather than for a diagnosed condition.

Can any of this be done remotely?

Remote psychology sessions are well established and work well for many presentations.
