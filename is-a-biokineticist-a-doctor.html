Guide

Is a biokineticist a doctor?

No - and the honest answer is more interesting than the short one.

A biokineticist is not a medical doctor. They do not diagnose disease, they do not prescribe medication, and they are not trained in medicine. The question comes up constantly though, and usually because someone has been referred to one and is trying to work out what they are walking into.

What a biokineticist is: a healthcare professional registered with the Health Professions Council of South Africa, who uses exercise clinically. The qualification is a four-year degree plus an internship, and the registration sits alongside physiotherapy, dietetics and psychology rather than alongside personal training.

What they actually do

Biokineticists prescribe and supervise exercise as a form of treatment. That covers the final phase of rehabilitation after injury or surgery, and the ongoing management of chronic conditions.

  • Rehabilitation after injury, once the acute phase has settled
  • Recovery and conditioning after surgery
  • Exercise programmes for diabetes and hypertension
  • Cardiac rehabilitation
  • Functional and postural assessment
  • Preventing an old injury from recurring

The distinction that matters

A doctor diagnoses and treats disease, and can prescribe. A biokineticist works within a scope defined by exercise, and refers back to a doctor when something falls outside it.

That boundary is not a limitation so much as the point. A biokineticist is the person you want once a diagnosis exists and the question has become how to rebuild strength and function safely.

Why people confuse the two

Partly the title - “biokineticist” sounds medical in a way “personal trainer” does not, and it should, because the registration is real. Partly because they work in clinical settings and take referrals from doctors.

The practical consequence is that a biokineticist is an appropriate person to be referred to, and not an appropriate person to diagnose the problem in the first place.

When a biokineticist is the right person

  • You have a diagnosis and need to rebuild strength or function
  • An old injury keeps returning
  • You have been told exercise is part of managing a chronic condition
  • You are recovering from surgery and past the acute phase
  • You want structured conditioning rather than a gym programme

Questions

Common questions

Is a biokineticist a doctor?

No. A biokineticist is a healthcare professional registered with the HPCSA who uses exercise clinically. They do not diagnose disease or prescribe medication.

Are biokineticists registered in South Africa?

Yes, with the Health Professions Council of South Africa.

Do I need a referral to see one?

Not necessarily, though a medical assessment often comes first where the underlying problem has not been diagnosed.

Is a biokineticist the same as a personal trainer?

No. The training, registration and scope are different, and biokineticists work clinically with injury and chronic disease.
