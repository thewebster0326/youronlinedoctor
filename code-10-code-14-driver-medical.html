Driver Medicals

Code 10 and Code 14 driver medicals

Your licence code and your Professional Driving Permit are two different things. The confusion between them costs drivers a lot of wasted trips.

Drivers routinely use “code 10”, “code 14” and “PDP” as if they were the same thing. They are not, and the difference decides whether you need a medical.

Your licence code describes what size and type of vehicle you are licensed to drive. A Professional Driving Permit is a separate authorisation that allows you to drive professionally - carrying goods or passengers for reward, or carrying dangerous goods. You can hold a code 14 licence and still not be permitted to drive that vehicle for a living without the permit.

The medical assessment attaches to the permit, not to the licence code. So the question is not really “does code 14 need a medical” - it is whether you are driving professionally.

Licence code versus permit

In broad terms, the heavier the vehicle, the higher the code. Code 10 covers larger goods vehicles, and code 14 covers the heaviest combinations including articulated vehicles.

The permit sits on top of that. It is what makes driving those vehicles for an employer or for reward lawful, and it is the permit application that requires the medical certificate.

Who ends up needing a medical

If you are driving professionally, you need the permit, and the permit needs the medical. That covers most drivers of heavy vehicles, because very few people hold a code 14 licence for personal use.

  • Long-haul and distribution truck drivers
  • Articulated and abnormal load drivers
  • Bus, coach and shuttle drivers
  • Tanker and dangerous goods drivers
  • Plant and construction vehicle drivers on public roads
  • Any driver whose employer requires a current permit

What the assessment covers

The same examination applies regardless of the code you hold: vision, hearing, blood pressure and general health, together with questions about chronic conditions and medication that could affect driving safely. There is no separate, harsher medical for code 14.

Fleet and employer assessments

Employers running fleets often need several drivers assessed at once, and need the certificates to be consistent and correctly completed.

If you are arranging assessments for a group of drivers rather than for yourself, get in touch and we will work out the most practical way to do it, including where mobile services can come to the depot.

Renewals

A permit does not last indefinitely, and a fresh medical is part of renewing it. Drivers most often get caught out by leaving it until the permit has already lapsed, which can mean time off the road.

Renewal periods and how recent your certificate must be are set by the authorities and can change. Check the current position with your Driving Licence Testing Centre, and book the medical before the deadline rather than after it.

What to bring

  • Your identity document
  • Your current driving licence showing the code
  • Your existing permit, if you are renewing
  • Glasses or contact lenses, if you use them
  • A list of medication you take regularly

Arranging it

Send a WhatsApp message with your area and we will confirm a time and a place. Where mobile doctor services cover your location, the assessment can be arranged where you are.

Application steps, fees and how recent a certificate must be are set by the licensing authorities and can change. Confirm the current requirements with your Driving Licence Testing Centre.

Questions

Common questions

Is a code 10 licence the same as a PDP?

No. The licence code is what you are licensed to drive. The Professional Driving Permit is a separate authorisation to drive professionally, and it is the permit that requires a medical certificate.

Do I need a medical for a code 14 licence?

The medical attaches to the Professional Driving Permit rather than the licence code. In practice almost every code 14 driver is driving professionally and therefore needs one.

Can my employer arrange medicals for several drivers?

Yes. Get in touch and we will work out the most practical arrangement, including depot visits where mobile services cover the area.

Can this be done online?

No. It involves a physical examination including vision and hearing, so it is conducted in person.
